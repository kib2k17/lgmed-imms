1. Summary

The proposal in one paragraph

We add an AI Assistant to the public website of LGMED-IMMS. A visitor (a citizen, an LGU staff member, a partner agency) types a question in their own words, such as “What programs does LGMED run in Agusan del Sur?” or “Paano mag-request ng dokumento?” The assistant looks through the information the Division has already published on the site (announcements, programs, frontline services, accomplishments, statistics, reports, documents and the calendar) and replies straight away in plain language, with links to the pages it used.

Why do this

  • Visitors find answers faster. They don't have to know which page to open; they just ask.
  • Fewer routine enquiries. Questions like “what are your office hours”, “what are the requirements for…” and “when is the next activity” are answered without a staff member having to reply.
  • Answers come from our own records. The assistant only uses what the Division has approved for publication, and every answer links to its source, so anyone can check it.
  • Always up to date. When a record is published or changed in IMMS, the assistant uses the new version. Nobody has to re-train or re-teach it.
  • It builds on what we have. The public website and its data already exist in IMMS. The assistant is a new feature on top of them, not a new system.

What we recommend

  1. Start with a public assistant that answers only from published information.
  2. Run it on a hosted AI service first (Option A in Section 6). This needs no new hardware, gives the best answer quality, and is cheap at the volume we expect. Because the assistant only reads information that is already public, sending it to an outside service exposes nothing new.
  3. Build it so the AI engine can be swapped. If the team later wants an assistant for internal records, which must stay in the office, we buy a local AI workstation (Section 7) and switch to it without rebuilding.
  4. Run a four-week staff-only pilot before the assistant goes public.

2. What an LLM is, in plain terms

A large language model (LLM) is the technology behind tools such as ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. It has read a very large amount of text and learned how language works, so it can understand a question written in ordinary words and write a clear answer.

On its own, an LLM knows a lot about the world in general but nothing about our Division: not our programs, our LGUs, our announcements or our schedule. If you ask it about them without giving it our information, it may guess, and a guess can sound confident and still be wrong. That is called a “hallucination”.

How we make it answer from our data

We do not re-train the model on our data. That is expensive, slow, and would leave it out of date the moment a record changes. We use a method called retrieval-augmented generation (RAG):

  1. When a question comes in, IMMS first searches our published records for the passages most likely to answer it.
  2. IMMS hands those passages to the LLM together with the question and a strict instruction: “Answer only from these passages. If they do not contain the answer, say so and point the person to the contact page.”
  3. The LLM writes the answer and IMMS attaches links to the pages it used.

An easy way to explain it: the LLM is like a capable new staff member who writes and speaks well but has never worked at LGMED. RAG is handing them the right folder before each question and telling them to answer only from what is in it.

3. What the assistant will do

For the public (Phase 1 — this proposal)

A chat button appears on every page of the public website. A visitor clicks it, types a question, and gets an answer in a few seconds. The visitor does not need an account.

LGMED AI Assistant

LGMED currently publishes three programs that involve barangays:

For the latest schedule of activities, see the Calendar. Sources: Programs › [Program A] · Programs › [Program B] · Calendar

Languages

The assistant answers in the language the visitor uses: English, Filipino, and Cebuano/Bisaya. Hosted models handle all three well. Smaller local models are noticeably weaker in Filipino and Cebuano (see Section 6).

Later phases (for a separate decision)

  • Staff assistant (inside IMMS, signed-in users only): summarise an incoming document, draft a routing note, draft the weekly accomplishment narrative, or ask “which LGUs have not yet submitted X?”. A person always reviews and approves what it drafts.
  • Because this works with internal, unpublished records, it has stricter privacy requirements and is the main reason to consider local hardware (Section 7).

4. How it works

Step by step

  1. Indexing (automatic, in the background). Whenever a record is published, changed or unpublished in IMMS, it is cut into short passages and stored in a search index. Unpublishing a record removes it from the index straight away.
  2. Search. When a question arrives, IMMS finds the handful of passages that best match its meaning, not just its words, so “requirements” also finds “documents needed”.
  3. Prepare. IMMS sends the LLM the question, those passages and fixed rules: answer only from the passages, never invent figures, names or dates, reply in the visitor's language, and keep it short.
  4. Answer. The LLM writes the reply. IMMS adds links to the source pages, records the exchange in a log (Section 8), and shows the answer to the visitor.

7. Hardware

If we use a hosted service (Option A)

No new hardware is needed. The computer that already runs IMMS handles the search and the chat. The heavy work happens at the provider. The only requirement is a stable internet connection on the server.

If we run the model ourselves (Option B or C)

An LLM runs mainly on the graphics card (GPU), and the most important number is the GPU's memory (VRAM): it limits how large, and therefore how capable, a model the machine can run. An ordinary office PC without a dedicated graphics card can technically run a small model, but too slowly for a public website.

8. Data privacy and safeguards

Data Privacy Act (RA 10173)

  • Consult the Data Protection Officer (DPO) before the pilot and before going public. A short privacy impact assessment is recommended.
  • Tell visitors. The chat window shows a short notice: they are talking to an AI, the answer may be wrong, they should not type personal information, and questions are logged to improve the service. It links to the site's existing Privacy statement, which gets a new paragraph about the assistant.
  • Keep only what we need. Logs keep the question, answer, sources used and time. IP addresses are kept only briefly, for abuse prevention. Logs are deleted after a set period (proposed: 90 days).
  • Hosted provider terms. Use a business/API account whose terms say our data is not used to train the provider's models, with the shortest available retention.

Built-in safeguards

SafeguardWhat it does
Published records onlyThe assistant cannot see internal modules (Section 5).
Answers must cite sourcesEvery answer links to its pages. If nothing relevant is found, it says so and points to the Contact page instead of guessing.
Fixed rulesIt never gives legal rulings, never comments on politics or individual officials, never makes promises on behalf of the Division, and refuses unrelated tasks.
Disclaimer“This is an AI assistant. Official information is on the linked pages and in issued documents.”
Personal data filterObvious personal details (mobile numbers, email addresses) in questions are masked before they are logged or sent out.
Abuse protectionA limit on questions per visitor per hour, a maximum question length, and reCAPTCHA (IMMS already supports it for sign-in) if abuse appears.
Spending cap(Hosted) A monthly budget limit at the provider. When it is reached, the assistant turns itself off and shows the Contact page.
Kill switchAn administrator can turn the assistant off instantly from Public Website settings.
Human reviewA staff member reviews a sample of conversations each week and flags wrong answers. These usually show missing or unclear published information, which is fixed at the source.

9. Costs

Option A — Hosted

  • Hardware: none.
  • Usage: providers charge by the amount of text processed. Each question costs a small fraction of a peso to a few pesos, depending on the model chosen and how long the answers are. The monthly total depends on how many questions visitors ask.
  • How we control it: during the pilot we record the real cost per question, then set a monthly cap at the provider. A smaller, cheaper model from the same provider is usually enough for this kind of question-and-answer.
  • Payment: providers bill by credit card or invoice in US dollars. Finance should confirm how this can be procured (for example, as a subscription service).

How we will measure success

  • Accuracy: share of reviewed answers that are correct, or correctly say “I don't know”. Target: 90% or more.
  • Usefulness: the “Was this helpful?” thumbs up/down in the chat window.
  • Fewer routine enquiries by phone, email and Facebook (compared with the months before).
  • Content gaps found: topics people ask about that we had not published.
  • Cost per month within the approved cap.

11. Risks and how we handle them

RiskLikelihoodHow we handle it
The assistant gives a wrong answerMediumIt answers only from passages, must cite them, and says “I don't know” otherwise. The disclaimer, weekly review and staff pilot also help.
Someone tries to trick it into saying something improperMediumFixed rules, refusal of off-topic requests, a length limit, logging and the kill switch. It has no access to anything that is not already public.
A visitor types personal informationMediumA notice not to, masking of obvious personal details, short log retention, and provider terms that rule out training on our data.
Costs grow unexpectedlyLowA monthly cap at the provider, a per-visitor limit, and automatic shut-off at the cap.
Provider outage or internet lossLowThe chat window says the assistant is unavailable and points to the Contact page. The rest of the website keeps working.
Published information is outdated, so the answers are tooMediumThe assistant is only as good as what we publish. The log of unanswered questions shows what needs updating.
The public mistakes an AI answer for an official statementLowA visible AI label, the disclaimer, and links to official pages in every answer.

13. Glossary

AI assistant / chatbotA chat window where people type questions and get written answers.
LLM (large language model)The AI technology that understands and writes language, e.g. Claude, GPT, Gemini, Llama.
HallucinationWhen an LLM states something false with confidence. Answering only from our passages is how we prevent it.
RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)Searching our own records first, then having the LLM answer only from what was found.
IndexA searchable copy of the published records, cut into short passages, kept up to date automatically.
Hosted service / APIAn LLM run by a provider on their servers, used over the internet and paid per use.
Local / open modelAn LLM whose files can be downloaded and run on our own computer, e.g. Llama, Qwen, Gemma.
Parameters (e.g. “8B”, “32B”)A rough measure of a model's size. Larger models are usually more capable but need more GPU memory.
GPU / VRAMThe graphics card and its memory. The main hardware an LLM runs on; VRAM limits the model size.
Ollama / vLLMFree programs for running open models on our own hardware.
DPOData Protection Officer, who advises on compliance with the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173).
