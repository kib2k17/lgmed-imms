LGMED-iMMS identity

Four logo concepts for the Integrated Monitoring and Management System, a recommendation, and the finished logo set for the login screen, dashboard, sidebar, reports, slides and the installed app.

Part 1

Four concepts

Each concept is shown large and at the 32 px and 16 px sizes of a browser tab, where most logos fail first.

32 / 16 px

Concept 1 · Modern government technology

Provincial Network

Inspiration
Caraga's five provinces (Agusan del Norte, Agusan del Sur, Dinagat Islands, Surigao del Norte and Surigao del Sur) reporting into one regional office.
Meaning
A shield outline holds a five-point network around a gold hub. The spokes are reporting lines. The outer ring is province-to-province collaboration.
Colour
Navy shield for institutional trust, royal blue nodes for the digital link, a gold hub for the regional office as the point of service.
Typography
Pairs with an ExtraBold sans wordmark. The open drawing needs a heavy wordmark to balance it.
Fit for government systems
Strong story and clearly governmental. The fine spokes fill in below 32 px, and network constellations are common in IT branding, so it is hard to own.
32 / 16 px

Concept 2 · Minimalist digital identity

Pulse Monogram Recommended

Inspiration
The letters of the name itself. The M is drawn as a trend line that rises, dips and rises again, the shape every monitoring chart makes.
Meaning
One continuous line writes “iM”. Its five vertices are nodes, one per province. The i’s gold dot stands for information and insight, set level with the two peaks.
Colour
Navy ground for authority, white line for transparency, a single gold dot for excellence in public service.
Typography
Inter, the system’s own interface face, so the logo and the screens it sits on share one voice.
Fit for government systems
Survives at 16 px, belongs to this name and no other, and is quick to draw. On its own the rounded tile reads like any app icon, which the refinement addresses.
32 / 16 px

Concept 3 · Smart governance

Assurance Ring

Inspiration
Dashboard gauges and the validation tick that closes a monitoring cycle.
Meaning
A progress ring fills toward compliance, a gold segment marks the target reached, and a tick confirms a validated submission.
Colour
Royal blue progress, gold achievement, navy check for the final, accountable sign-off.
Typography
Works with a medium-weight wordmark. The ring is already heavy.
Fit for government systems
Says “monitoring” instantly, but a tick in a circle is used by verification badges and task apps everywhere. Low ownership.
32 / 16 px

Concept 4 · Premium institutional branding

Ledger Seal

Inspiration
Bound ledgers and filed records: the document side of the system (incoming, outgoing, submissions, reports).
Meaning
A hexagonal seal with three stacked plates. Records are layered and integrated, and the gold top plate is the validated record of truth.
Colour
Navy seal for permanence, white and light-blue layers for organised information, gold for the authoritative record.
Typography
Suits a wide-tracked capitals wordmark for letterheads and reports.
Fit for government systems
Timeless and at home on official documents. The layer stack is the generic “database” glyph and says little about monitoring, and a hexagon carries less government association than a shield.

Recommendation

Concept 2, refined with the shield and the provinces from Concept 1

  1. Originality. The monogram is drawn from the system's own name, so no other agency or vendor can have it. Networks, ticks and layer stacks are shared by thousands of logos.
  2. Scalability. It is the only concept that stays legible at 16 px. A favicon cut with a heavier line and no node beads keeps it crisp in a browser tab.
  3. Meaning. In one line it says monitoring (the trend), connectivity (the nodes), information (the i) and Caraga (five nodes, five provinces).
  4. Interface fit. It is set in Inter, the interface face of LGMED-iMMS, and uses the navy and gold the screens already use.

The refinement puts the monogram on a split shield: a navy half and a royal-blue half joined down the centre, for the regional office and the LGUs working as one. The shield gives the mark the institutional weight the plain app tile lacked. It is drawn plainly, with no seal-like detail, so it can never be mistaken for the DILG seal.

Part 6

Using the logo

Clear space and minimum size

  • Keep clear space of at least one quarter of the shield's height on every side.
  • Horizontal logo with tagline: 240 px or 60 mm wide minimum. Below that, use the compact logo.
  • Compact logo: 120 px or 30 mm wide minimum.
  • Icon: 24 px and up. Use favicon.svg at 16–23 px.

Do not

  • Recolour, stretch, rotate or outline the logo, or add shadows or gradients.
  • Retype the wordmark in another font, or rearrange LGMED and iMMS.
  • Place the colour logo on photos or mid-tone backgrounds. Use the white monochrome version.
  • Add a second gold element to a lock-up.
  • Use the dark versions on light backgrounds, or the reverse.

Alongside the DILG seal

  • The official DILG seal is the department's identity. The LGMED-iMMS logo identifies only this system and never replaces the seal.
  • When both appear, the seal comes first (left or top), followed by a thin divider and then the system logo, at a similar visual height.
  • Never place the mark inside the seal, merge the two into one emblem, or use the shield as a stand-in for the seal on official issuances.
