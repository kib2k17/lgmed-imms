LGMED-iMMS identity · Round two

Five alternative logo directions

Each option is a different idea from the shield now in the system, set with the same LGMED-iMMS wordmark so the symbols can be compared fairly. Each is shown in its light and dark lock-ups, at browser-tab sizes, and as the navy app tile used for the installed app.

Previous logo: the Pulse Shield. Option E, Rising Sun, was adopted on 27 October 2026 and is now the logo across the system.

Option A · Document management, the Philippine sun

Open Ledger

64 / 32 / 16
What it means
Two record pages stand open and their top edges form the M. The navy page is the regional office and the royal-blue page is the LGUs, meeting at the fold. The gold disc in the fold is the dot of the i, and it also recalls the sun of the Philippine flag.
Strength
The most distinctive silhouette of the three. It reads as an M even at 16 px, and it says government records without using a shield or seal shape.
Trade-off
It says less about monitoring than about records. The monitoring story has to come from the wordmark and the system itself.

Option B · Monitoring and evaluation, plainly

Rising Index

64 / 32 / 16
What it means
Four bars rise in sequence: an indicator improving period after period. The first bar is the i, with its gold dot as the data point being tracked.
Strength
The clearest of the three at 16 px and the most literal fit for a monitoring division. The rounded tile matches app-store and PWA conventions.
Trade-off
Rising bars are close to the familiar signal-strength and analytics icons, so it is the least ownable. It also carries the least institutional weight.

Option C · Monitoring and evaluation as focus

Evaluation Frame

64 / 32 / 16
What it means
An L, for LGMED and local government, and its mirror in royal blue close around a gold point. The regional office and the LGUs frame the one result being evaluated.
Strength
Bold and simple, with two strokes and a dot. It holds up at 16 px, carries the L monogram, and has a confident, modern feel without any badge.
Trade-off
Corner brackets can recall a crop or focus tool. The two colours and the rounded joins keep it from looking like a stock icon, but it is less ornamental than A.

Option D · The dashboard, made into a letter

Data Mosaic

64 / 32 / 16
What it means
An M picked out of a 5×5 grid of tiles, the grid of a dashboard. The navy side is the regional office, the royal-blue side is the LGUs, and the single gold tile at the centre of the M is the information both work from.
Strength
Unmistakably digital and unique to the name. It looks at home in the system's tiled dashboard and on the app icon.
Trade-off
The pale empty tiles turn into texture below 24 px, so the favicon would need a cut without them. It is the busiest of the five.

Option E · Progress, with a Philippine accent

Rising Sun Adopted

64 / 32 / 16
What it means
A gold sun rises over a horizon line. Its five rays grow longer from left to right like a performance trend, and the last ray, in royal blue, is the result reached.
Strength
The warmest and most public-facing of the five. The sun gives a clear Philippine reference without copying the flag or the DILG seal.
Trade-off
Sunrise marks are popular, so the lengthening rays carry the distinctiveness. Its wide, low shape sits smaller than the others in a square icon.

Recommendation

Shortlist: A Open Ledger and C Evaluation Frame

Open Ledger has the most distinctive silhouette. Evaluation Frame is the boldest and simplest, and the best at 16 px. Both work without a badge or seal shape. Data Mosaic is the most digital, but it needs a simplified favicon. Rising Sun is the warmest choice for public-facing material. Rising Index is clear, but it is the easiest to mistake for a stock icon.

An earlier ring design was dropped because it resembled the Messenger logo. Every option was checked against well-known app icons, including chat, signal-strength and brightness icons.
